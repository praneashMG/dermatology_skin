Dermatological Photoprotection Standard

The Clinical Sun Protection & UV Defense Guide

Over 80% of preventable premature facial aging and nearly 90% of non-melanoma skin cancers are directly attributable to cumulative ultraviolet radiation. Here is the evidence-based protocol our dermatologists mandate for every patient.

UVA Rays (320–400 nm)

Aging & Deep Elastosis

Accounts for 95% of UV radiation reaching Earth. UVA penetrates cloud cover, office glass, and car windshields directly into the deep dermis, breaking down collagen and driving melasma formation. Look for Broad Spectrum PA++++ ratings.

UVB Rays (290–320 nm)

Burning & Direct DNA Damage

High energy waves that cause acute erythema (sunburn) and direct thymine dimer mutations in epidermal keratinocyte DNA. Responsible for basal cell, squamous cell carcinoma, and melanoma. SPF measures protection predominantly against UVB rays.

HEV Blue Light & IR-A

Pigmentation in Fitz IV-VI

High Energy Visible light emitted by solar rays and screens triggers sustained, stubborn hyperpigmentation, especially in Fitzpatrick skin types IV through VI. Only tinted mineral sunscreens containing Iron Oxides block blue light.

The 4 Non-Negotiable Dermatologist Application Rules

1

The "Two-Finger" Dose

Most people apply only 25–50% of the tested amount. Dispense two full finger lengths (index and middle fingers) of sunscreen to adequately cover the face, neck, and ears at 2mg/cm².

2

Mineral vs. Chemical

For melasma, rosacea, and post-laser recovery, use 100% Zinc Oxide / Titanium Dioxide filters that reflect heat rather than absorbing and converting UV energy into skin-warming heat.

3

The 2-Hour Reset

Organic filters degrade and mineral shields rub off from sebum and perspiration. Reapply every 2 hours during direct outdoor exposure or immediately after 80 minutes of swimming.

4

365-Day Protocol

UVA levels remain consistent through winter overcast and rain. Daily photoprotection is the single most cost-effective anti-aging and preventative medical step in dermatology.
